Push notifications

What a buzz looks like on each OS, how we ask for the permission, and what the Notify me row does once it is real. The two OS surfaces are drawn in Apple's and Google's own design languages rather than ours, because that is what the user actually sees: on a lock screen we control four things only, the icon, the title, the body, and on Android the channel.

One · iOS

Lock screen and banner

iOS gives us a title line and a body line inside its own material. The app name is forced uppercase by the system and the timestamp is the system's. Everything expressive we might want to do, tier colour, the source mark, the ring, is unavailable here, which is the argument for the message being a sentence rather than a label: "Priya Menon needs you" carries more than a coloured dot ever could on a surface we do not own.

Two · Android

Heads-up, lock screen, drawer

Android renders the same two strings inside a Material 3 card, and adds one thing iOS does not have: the channel. A channel is a user-facing bucket with its own importance, sound and vibration, and once created its importance belongs to the user forever. We create exactly one, urgent, at IMPORTANCE_HIGH, so the notification arrives as a heads-up rather than a silent drawer entry. Shipping without a channel files us under the system "Default" channel, which the Expo docs explicitly warn against.

Three · Asking

Priming first, OS prompt second

The OS prompt is a one-shot resource. On iOS a denial is close to permanent: the user has to find Settings, scroll to the app, and flip a switch they have no reason to look for. So the OS prompt is never the user's first contact with the idea. Our own screen goes first, and only "Turn them on" fires the system dialog. "Not now" costs nothing, because it leaves the OS permission unasked and we can offer it again from the You tab.

Placement: the first time a source finishes connecting, not at signup. Asking before there is a feed is asking somebody to consent to notifications about nothing. Straight after a connect, the user has just watched the product read their actual work, and the promise below is a claim they can evaluate. It follows the pattern the name modal already uses, fired once and never re-nagged.

Five · Tap

Where a tap lands

Every message carries data.item_id. A tap opens that item's detail sheet directly, not the feed with the item somewhere in it. A batch carries data.item_ids and opens the feed scrolled to the top, because there is no single right item to open.

Six · What is not drawn

Out of scope, deliberately

Notification actions (Reply, Snooze from the shade). Drawn once above to show what Android allows, but they need a background task and a signed action route. Follow-on.
Badges. An unread count on the icon is a different promise from "this needs you", and a badge that never clears is its own kind of noise.
Rich content, images, custom sounds. Nothing here needs them.
Quiet hours and per-source rules. The four levels are the whole surface for now.
iOS delivery verification. The code ships iOS-ready, but APNs needs the paid Apple Developer account. Until then, every real-device check happens on an Android preview build.
